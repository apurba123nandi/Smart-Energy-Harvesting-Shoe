# 👟 Piezoelectric Energy Harvesting Shoes

A wearable energy-harvesting project that converts the mechanical energy generated while walking into electrical energy using piezoelectric elements embedded inside a shoe sole.

## 📌 Overview

Every step we take produces mechanical energy through the pressure applied by our body weight to the ground. This project explores how that normally wasted mechanical energy can be converted into electrical energy.

The system uses **piezoelectric discs/elements** placed inside the shoe. When the user walks, the pressure generated by each footstep causes the piezoelectric elements to deform and generate an electrical output.

The generated electrical energy can then be rectified, stored and used for low-power applications.

Piezoelectric materials generate electrical charge when subjected to mechanical stress, making them suitable for energy-harvesting applications such as footwear.

## ⚡ Working Principle

The project works according to the **piezoelectric effect**.

When mechanical pressure is applied to a piezoelectric material, electrical charge is produced.

The basic energy conversion is:

**Mechanical Energy → Piezoelectric Element → Electrical Energy → Rectification → Energy Storage → Load**

When a person steps on the shoe:

1. The person's body weight applies pressure to the sole.
2. The pressure is transferred to the piezoelectric elements.
3. The piezoelectric elements generate electrical pulses.
4. The generated output is passed through a rectification circuit.
5. The rectified electrical energy can be stored in a rechargeable battery or capacitor.
6. The stored energy can be supplied to a low-power electronic load.

Piezoelectric plates/discs have two terminals and produce voltage when mechanical force is applied.

## 🔧 Circuit Concept

Multiple piezoelectric elements can be installed throughout the shoe sole so that different areas of the foot apply pressure to different elements.

A basic architecture is:

```text
Piezoelectric Elements
        ↓
AC/Pulsed Electrical Output
        ↓
Bridge Rectifier
        ↓
Filter Capacitor
        ↓
Voltage Regulation
        ↓
Rechargeable Battery / Supercapacitor
        ↓
Low-Power Load
```

The exact circuit depends on the type and number of piezoelectric elements and the required output.

---

## 👣 Placement of Piezoelectric Elements

Piezoelectric elements can be positioned in areas where significant pressure is generated during walking, such as:

* Heel
* Forefoot
* Ball of the foot
* Toe area

The elements should be mechanically protected because repeated walking produces continuous impact and bending forces.

A suitable insole or protective layer can be placed above the elements to distribute pressure and improve comfort.

---

## 🔌 Connecting Multiple Piezoelectric Elements

Multiple piezoelectric elements can be connected together to increase the available electrical output.

Possible configurations include:

### Parallel Connection

Used when the objective is to increase the available current/charge contribution.

```text
Piezo 1 ─┐
Piezo 2 ─┼──→ Rectifier → Storage
Piezo 3 ─┤
Piezo 4 ─┘
```

### Series Connection

Used when a higher voltage is desired.

```text
Piezo 1 → Piezo 2 → Piezo 3 → Piezo 4
                         ↓
                     Rectifier
                         ↓
                      Storage
```

The best configuration depends on the electrical characteristics of the selected piezoelectric elements.

---

## 🔋 Energy Storage

The output produced by piezoelectric elements is not a steady DC supply. Walking produces intermittent electrical pulses.

Therefore, the generated energy should be conditioned and stored before being supplied to an electronic device.

Possible storage components include:

* Rechargeable battery
* Supercapacitor
* Capacitor

A rectifier is generally required to convert the piezoelectric output into a suitable polarity before storage.

* LED lighting
* Small sensors

* Step counters
* Environmental sensors

Research on piezoelectric footwear focuses on harvesting energy from walking and running for practical low-power applications.

## 🛠️ Prototype Development

### Step 1 — Select the Shoe

Choose a comfortable shoe with enough space inside the sole or insole to accommodate the piezoelectric elements and wiring.

### Step 2 — Prepare the Insole

Create appropriate locations for the piezoelectric elements.

### Step 3 — Install the Piezoelectric Elements

Place the elements in high-pressure areas of the sole.

### Step 4 — Connect the Elements

Connect the piezoelectric elements according to the required electrical configuration.

### Step 5 — Add the Rectifier

Connect the piezoelectric output to a rectifier circuit.

### Step 6 — Add Energy Storage

Connect the rectified output to a capacitor, supercapacitor or rechargeable battery through appropriate charging/regulation circuitry.

### Step 7 — Add the Load

Connect a low-power electronic device to the regulated output.

Walk normally and measure:

* Voltage
* Current
* Power

* Charging time
* Output under different walking conditions

The prototype should generate electrical pulses whenever pressure is applied to the piezoelectric elements.

## ⚠️ Limitations

* Individual footsteps produce relatively small amounts of energy.
* Output is intermittent.
* Energy storage and conversion introduce losses.
* Piezoelectric elements can be damaged by excessive mechanical stress.
* Adding components can affect shoe comfort and weight.
* High-power devices cannot normally be powered directly from a small number of piezoelectric elements.

A piezoelectric shoe should therefore be designed primarily around **energy harvesting for low-power electronics**, not as a direct replacement for a conventional phone charger.
