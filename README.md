# 👟 Piezoelectric Smart Shoes – Energy Generation from Walking

## 📌 Overview

This project demonstrates how electrical energy can be generated from the mechanical pressure produced while walking.

The system uses **piezoelectric discs placed inside the sole of a shoe**. When a person walks, the pressure applied to the piezoelectric elements produces an electrical voltage. The generated electrical output can be collected, rectified, stored, and used for small electrical applications.

The project demonstrates the practical application of the **piezoelectric effect** and explores how human movement can be converted into electrical energy.

---

## 🎯 Objective

The main objectives of this project are:

* To generate electrical energy from human footsteps.
* To demonstrate the piezoelectric effect.
* To convert mechanical energy from walking into electrical energy.
* To collect and store the generated electrical energy.
* To explore energy harvesting using wearable technology.
* To demonstrate a simple renewable energy generation concept.

---

## ⚙️ Working Principle

The project works on the **piezoelectric effect**.

Piezoelectric materials generate an electrical charge when mechanical pressure or stress is applied to them.

When a person walks:

1. The person's body weight creates pressure on the shoe sole.
2. The pressure is transferred to the piezoelectric discs.
3. The piezoelectric discs generate electrical voltage.
4. The electrical outputs from the piezoelectric elements are collected.
5. The generated electrical output is rectified to obtain a usable polarity.
6. The electrical energy can be stored using a capacitor or other suitable storage element.
7. The stored energy can be used to power a small electrical load.

### Energy Conversion

```text
Mechanical Energy
       ↓
   Footstep
       ↓
Pressure on Piezoelectric Discs
       ↓
Electrical Energy
       ↓
Rectification
       ↓
Energy Storage
       ↓
Electrical Output
```

## 👣 Piezoelectric Disc Placement

The piezoelectric discs are positioned inside the shoe sole in areas where pressure is generated during walking.

Typical high-pressure areas include:

* Heel
* Ball of the foot
* Forefoot
* Toe area

The exact placement can be adjusted according to the shoe design and the pressure distribution of the user's foot.

A protective layer should be placed over the piezoelectric elements to prevent direct damage from repeated impacts.

## 🔌 Circuit

The basic electrical arrangement is:

```text
Piezoelectric Discs
       │
       ↓
Electrical Output
       │
       ↓
Rectifier Circuit
       │
       ↓
Capacitor
       │
       ↓
LED / Small Load
```

Multiple piezoelectric elements can be connected together to collect the energy generated from different areas of the shoe.

The connection configuration can be selected depending on whether higher voltage or greater current contribution is required.

---

## 🔋 Energy Storage

The electrical output generated by a piezoelectric element is produced as the user applies pressure to the shoe.

Because the output is intermittent, a capacitor can be used to collect and temporarily store the generated electrical energy.

The stored energy can then be released to a small electrical load.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations

* The amount of energy generated from individual footsteps is relatively small.
* The output is intermittent rather than continuous.
* Energy is lost during rectification and storage.
* Piezoelectric discs can be damaged by excessive mechanical stress.
* Additional components can affect shoe comfort.
* The basic system is suitable mainly for low-power applications.

The project should therefore be considered an **energy-harvesting prototype**, rather than a replacement for conventional power sources.
